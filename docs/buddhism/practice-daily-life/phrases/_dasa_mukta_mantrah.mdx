### Daśa Mukta Mantrāḥ

<details>
  <summary>
    Daśa Mukta Mantrāḥ, the Ten Liberation Prayers
    ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 十解脱咒), is a collection
    of [esoteric Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajrayana) prayers recited for those near
    the end of life. The ten are:

    > 1. **[Amoghapāśa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalokite%C5%9Bvara#Manifestations)** Padma
         Uṣṇīṣa ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 白蓮花頂髻無垢密咒):
         the lotus-crown dhāraṇī of Amoghapāśa Avalokiteśvara, and the tenth of the ten lotus-crown
         mantras in the 不空羂索神變真言經 (T1092), translated by Bodhiruci between 707 and 709. The
         mantra is <code>{amoghapasaPadmaUsnisaPhrase.transliteration.children}</code>; it is
         written out and posted over a doorway so that anyone passing beneath it receives the
         blessing, and it is also used on funeral banners and in the consecration of images.
    > 2. **[Darśana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darshan_(Indian_religions)#Mahayana_Buddhism)**
         Mukta ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 見即解脱咒):
         "liberation by seeing", a Tibetan prayer held to purify a hundred thousand eons of negative
         karma merely by being seen. Its name joins darśana, seeing, to mukta, freed. It comes down
         through the Tibetan lineage, which treasures it as a mantra to be seen, and it is widely
         known today through the mantra cards printed for that purpose. The form carried with it is
         <code>{darsanaMuktaPhrase.transliteration.children}</code>.
    > 3. **[Samantabhadra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantabhadra_(Bodhisattva)#Tibetan_Buddhism)**
         Ṣaḍja Mukta ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 听即解脱咒):
         "liberation by hearing", linked with the Six Spaces of Samantabhadra and the six realms.
         The six syllables {<Words {...sadjaMuktaPhrase} />} are traditionally said to free beings
         in any realm upon hearing or seeing them, and in Dzogchen they express six aspects of
         primordial awareness. The tradition traces it to a terma of Padmasambhava, and it has come
         into wide public circulation in recent times.
    > 4. **Mahāmaṇi Vipulavimāna**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 大寶廣博樓閣善住祕密陀羅尼經):
         the dhāraṇī of the jewelled palace, taught by Śākyamuni and translated by Amoghavajra as the
         大寶廣博樓閣善住祕密陀羅尼經 (T1005A). Reciting it erases the five inexpiable acts and the
         four grave offences, and it is used widely for the dead. At the moment of death the buddhas
         comfort the dying and lead them to a pure land, and even insects and birds touched by its
         shadow or dust are freed.
    > 5. **[Akṣobhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akshobhya)**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [阿閦佛](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E9%98%BF%E9%96%A6%E4%BD%9B)) Hṛdaya:
         the buddha of the eastern realm Abhirati, one of the Five Wisdom Buddhas. In the
         拔濟苦難陀羅尼經 (T1395), translated by Xuanzang, the Buddha proclaims his dhāraṇī - there under
         the name 不動如來 - for the benefit of beings; hearing it clears karmic obstruction including
         the five inexpiable acts, and sand, water, or white sesame blessed with the mantra and
         sprinkled on the dead is held to release them from the lower realms.
    > 6. **Ratnaśikhin**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [宝髻佛](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E5%AE%9D%E9%AB%BB%E4%BD%9B)) Hṛdaya:
         Jewel Crest, a buddha of the past whose vow is that anyone in the worlds of the ten
         directions who hears his name at the moment of death will be reborn as a god in
         Trāyastriṃśa. That vow is recorded in the 金光明最勝王經 (T0665), translated by Yijing in 703.
         His heart mantra is carried in the practice of reciting over water that holds insects, so
         that the creatures in it are freed.
    > 7. **[Bhaiṣajyaguru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaisajyaguru)**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [藥師佛](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%97%A5%E5%B8%AB%E4%BD%9B)) Hṛdaya:
         the Medicine Buddha of the eastern realm Vaiḍūrya. His scripture (T0450), translated by
         Xuanzang in 650, has the Buddha enter samādhi and send the 藥師灌頂真言 forth from the light of
         his uṣṇīṣa; reciting it heals illness and fulfils wishes, and those who hear his name are met
         at death by eight bodhisattvas who show them the way to his realm.
    > 8. **[Uṣṇīṣavijayā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usnisavijaya)**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [佛頂尊勝佛母](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E5%B0%8A%E5%8B%9D%E4%BD%9B%E9%A0%82)):
         the victorious-crown goddess who issued from the Buddha's uṣṇīṣa, and one of the three
         principal long-life deities of Vajrayāna. Her scripture (T0967), translated by Buddhapāli in
         676, was spoken for the god 善住, who had been told he would die in seven days; it purifies
         all evil destinies, and is the prayer most used for the dead, recited over the body and
         inscribed on the 尊勝幢.
    > 9. **[Avalokiteśvara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalokite%C5%9Bvara)**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [觀世音](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E8%A7%80%E4%B8%96%E9%9F%B3%E8%8F%A9%E8%96%A9)) Hṛdaya:
         the six-syllable mantra <code>{avalokitesvaraPhrase.transliteration.children}</code>, the
         heart of Avalokiteśvara, understood as Jewel-Lotus. It is the 六字大明陀羅尼 of the
         佛說大乘莊嚴寶王經 (T1050), a mantra that scripture calls difficult to encounter; the Buddha
         taught that reciting it while attending to the sound can lead to eight hundred samādhis,
         and that it purifies negative karma and prevents lower rebirth.
    > 10. **[Amitābha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amit%C4%81bha)**
          ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
          [阿彌陀佛](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E9%98%BF%E5%BC%A5%E9%99%80%E4%BD%9B)) Hṛdaya:
          Amitābha of Sukhāvatī, whose heart dhāraṇī is linked with longevity, discernment, and the
          purification of the aggregates. It carries the recognition of the emptiness of all
          phenomena, through which the aggregates are purified, and it is recited to support rebirth
          in the pure land.

    Reciting these prayers is held to aid the dying and the deceased. The Chinese title 十解脫咒, the
    Ten Liberation Prayers, gathers mantras that have come down through both the Chinese esoteric
    canon and the Tibetan terma traditions, and it follows the way these ten are recited together in
    practice.
