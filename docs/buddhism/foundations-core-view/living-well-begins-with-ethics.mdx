## Ethical Foundations

Living well in Buddhism begins with **ethical conduct**. Ethics are not rules
imposed by a god or authority - they are practical guidelines that prevent
harm, reduce future suffering, and keep the mind clear.

The Buddha taught the **[Five Precepts](#pañcaśīla)** ([Pañcaśīla](#pañcaśīla))
as simple, powerful ways to avoid creating new karmic debts.

<PancaSila />

## How Karma Works

Karma means **intentional action** - actions of body, speech, and mind shaped
by intention (*cetanā*).

> Cetanāhaṁ, bhikkhave, kammaṁ vadāmi. Cetayitvā kammaṁ karoti - kāyena,
> vācāya, manasā.
>
> It is intention that I call karma. Having intended, one acts by body, speech,
> or mind.
>
> -- [Nibbedhikasutta (AN 6.63), Aṅguttara Nikāya](https://suttacentral.net/an6.63/en/sujato)

This principle is the foundation of all karmic understanding.

Karma functions like a **seed**:

- intention plants it
- conditions nourish it
- results ripen later

It is not divine judgment or punishment - it is a **natural law of cause and
effect**.

### Good Karma, Bad Karma, and the Threshold ([Pañcasīla](#pañcaśīla))

A practical way to understand the quality of karma:

- **Breaking the [Five Precepts](#pañcaśīla):** unwholesome karma
- **Keeping the [Five Precepts](#pañcaśīla):** avoiding harmful karma
- **Actions done with kindness, generosity, or wisdom beyond the precepts:**
  wholesome karma

The precepts form the **basic threshold** for staying free from negative karma.
Anything practiced above that foundation naturally inclines toward good karma.

## Intention and Karmic Weight

The Buddha taught that the [**intention behind an action**](#how-karma-works)
determines its karmic weight. The same outward deed can carry very different
karmic significance depending on the mind behind it.

Consider generosity:

- If you have **$10** and give **$5**, you offer **half of everything you have**.
- If you have **$1 trillion** and give **$5**, the sacrifice is almost nothing.

Karmic weight depends not on the external amount, but on **intention, effort,
and the degree of personal sacrifice** involved.

---

## When Intention Is Weakened (Mental Illness and Karma)

Karma depends on [**clear intention**](#how-karma-works). When intention is
unclear, confused, or impaired, the karmic force of an action is naturally
weaker.

A person who cannot fully understand or intend their actions - for example due
to mental illness - generates **less karmic weight** than someone acting with
full awareness and deliberate intention.

This is one of the kinder aspects of the law of karma: where understanding is
limited, the consequences are likewise limited. The practice of ethics is meant
to be supported by a mind that can choose clearly, and the teachings hold
space for the times when that capacity is reduced.
