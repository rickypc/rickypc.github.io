### Daśa Atyaya Mantrāḥ

<Phrase {...phrase} {...props} />

<details>
  <summary>
    Daśa Atyaya Mantrāḥ, the Ten Prayers for the Dead
    ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 十臨終咒), is a collection
    of [esoteric Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajrayana) prayers recited for those at the
    threshold of death. The ten are:

    > 1. **[Ekādaśamukhalokeśvara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ek%C4%81da%C5%9Bamukha)**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [十一面觀音](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E5%8D%81%E4%B8%80%E9%9D%A2%E8%A7%80%E9%9F%B3)):
         the eleven-faced form of Avalokiteśvara, whose spell is taught in the 十一面神呪心經 (T1071),
         translated by Xuanzang. Its promised benefits are the ones the dying need most: the dying
         see the buddhas, are not reborn in the lower realms, and do not die from accident or
         hardship, while a single recitation erases the four root offences together with the five
         inexpiable acts.
    > 2. **[Uṣṇīṣavijayā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usnisavijaya)**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [佛頂尊勝佛母](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E5%B0%8A%E5%8B%9D%E4%BD%9B%E9%A0%82)): the
         victorious-crown goddess who issues from the Buddha's uṣṇīṣa, and one of the three
         principal long-life deities of Vajrayāna. Her scripture (T0967), translated by Buddhapāli
         in 676, gives the funeral rite directly. The dhāraṇī is recited twenty-one times over a
         handful of earth, which is scattered on the bones of the dead, and they are then reborn in
         the heavens.
    > 3. **[Milarepa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milarepa)**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [密勒日巴](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E5%AF%86%E5%8B%92%E6%97%A5%E5%B7%B4)): the
         Tibetan yogi and poet-saint (1040-1123) of the Kagyü lineage, invoked here as the guru of
         the lineage. His Hṛdaya is a guru-yoga heart mantra; the tradition holds that whoever
         thinks of him or prays to him has him present, and that those who see or hear of him are
         reborn in a pure land within seven lifetimes.
    > 4. **[Mahāvairocana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vairocana)**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [毗盧遮那佛](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E6%AF%97%E5%8D%A2%E9%81%AE%E9%82%A3%E4%BD%9B))
         with the
         [Sarvadurgatipariśodhana Dhāraṇī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vairocana#Mantras_and_Dharanis):
         the purification form of Vairocana, the
         [dharmakāya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmak%C4%81ya) and central figure of the Five
         Jinas. The tantra to which the dhāraṇī belongs is organised around the purification of
         karma through funerary ritual, its frame story being a god who has fallen into hell. The
         Tibetan translation (Toh 483) gives Sarvavid Vairocana as its maṇḍala deity, while the
         Chinese version (T0939), translated in 1001, gives the tathāgata 淨諸惡趣王如來.
    > 5. **[Bhaiṣajyaguru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaisajyaguru)**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [藥師佛](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%97%A5%E5%B8%AB%E4%BD%9B)) Hṛdaya: the Medicine
         Buddha of the eastern realm Vaiḍūrya. His scripture, the 藥師琉璃光如來本願功德經 (T0450),
         translated by Xuanzang, promises that those who hear his name are met at death by eight
         bodhisattvas who show them the way and are born in his realm, no longer to fall into the
         lower realms; the heart mantra itself, the 藥師灌頂真言, is spoken from the Buddha's uṣṇīṣa.
    > 6. **Raśmi Vimala Viśuddha Prabhā Nāma Dhāraṇī**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 無垢淨光大陀羅尼經):
         the "Stainless Rays of Pure Light" dhāraṇī, which purifies the five uninterrupted negative
         karmas. Its scripture (T1024) was translated by Mitraśānta in 704, and is centered on
         making stūpas. Writing the dhāraṇī out and placing it in a stūpa, and reciting it
         seventy-seven times over the name of the dead, frees the deceased from the lower realms and
         brings rebirth in the heavens or in Tuṣita.
    > 7. **Raśmi Vimala Viśuddha Prabhā Nāma Hṛdaya**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 遠塔陀羅尼): the
         heart-recitation that belongs with the longer dhāraṇī above, known in Tibetan as Özer drimé
         nyingpo, Heart of Stainless Rays. It is carried in the tradition under the Chinese name
         遠塔陀羅尼.
    > 8. **Vimala Uṣṇīṣa** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         無垢仏頂): the Stainless Crown, one of the seventeen uṣṇīṣa-rāja deities and a principal
         dhāraṇī of consecration. Its scripture (T1025), translated by Dānapāla, tells of a god in
         Tuṣita doomed to hell within seven days whom neither Indra nor the other gods can save, and
         whose rescue comes when the Buddha emits light from his uṣṇīṣa and teaches the dhāraṇī;
         writing it out and placing it in a stūpa is likened to enshrining the whole-body relics of
         countless tathāgatas.
    > 9. **[Amoghapāśa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalokite%C5%9Bvara#Manifestations)** Padma
         Uṣṇīṣa ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         白蓮花頂髻無垢密咒): the lotus-crown dhāraṇī of the Amoghapāśa series of Avalokiteśvara.
         Whoever sees, hears, remembers, or touches it is purified of all negativities and freed
         from rebirth in the lower realms. It is preserved within the larger 不空羂索神變真言經
         (T1092), translated by Bodhiruci, and 白蓮花頂髻無垢密咒 is the name it goes by in devotional
         use.
    > 10. **[Akṣobhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akshobhya)**
          ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
          [阿閦佛](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E9%98%BF%E9%96%A6%E4%BD%9B)): the buddha of the
          eastern realm Abhirati, one of the Five Wisdom Buddhas. The dhāraṇī bearing his name, in
          the 拔濟苦難陀羅尼經 (T1395) translated by Xuanzang, erases the five inexpiable acts together
          with the four grave offences and the slander of the Dharma, and promises that at death
          Akṣobhya and his bodhisattvas appear before the dying to welcome them to his pure land.

    The set is associated with rites for the dying, and reciting these prayers is held to support
    the dying and the recently deceased. The Chinese title 十臨終咒 gathers ten prayers that the
    tradition recites at the threshold of death, drawn from the funerary and purification liturgies
    in which each one stands.

    <em>Source: [CBETA](https://cbetaonline.dila.edu.tw/) - Taishō T1071, T0967, T0450, T1024, T1025, T1092, T1395</em>
