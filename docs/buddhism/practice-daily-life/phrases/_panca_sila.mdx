### Pañcaśīla

<Phrase {...phrase} {...props} />

<details>
  <summary>
    Pañcaśīla, the [five
    precepts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_precepts#In_P%C4%81li_tradition), are the basic
    training rules undertaken by lay Buddhists, consisting of abstaining from killing, stealing,
    sexual misconduct, false speech, and intoxication. They are the simplest way to avoid creating
    new karmic debts and serve as gentle reminders to stay kind and careful, and they are recited as
    a commitment to ethical conduct and non-harm.

    Keeping these precepts keeps the karmic balance sheet clean - fewer new debts, fewer
    complications, and a quieter heart, and it is traditionally held to protect the mind, reduce
    remorse, and support meditation.

    > 1. **Don't hurt living beings** (*pāṇātipātā veramaṇī*): every creature wants to live. Be
         gentle with people, animals, even tiny bugs.
    > 2. **Don't take what isn't yours** (*adinnādānā veramaṇī*): if it doesn't belong to you, leave
         it. Sharing is good, stealing is not.
    > 3. **Don't break trust in love** (*kāmesumicchācārā veramaṇī*): be honest and kind in
         friendships and relationships. Don't cause harm with your choices.
    > 4. **Don't lie** (*musāvādā veramaṇī*): words can heal or hurt. Speak the truth, and use words
         to help, not harm.
    > 5. **Don't cloud your mind** (*surāmerayamajjapamādaṭṭhānā veramaṇī*): stay clear and mindful.
         Avoid things that make you lose control or forget kindness.

    Each precept is a *veramaṇī*, an abstention taken on as a training rule. The suttas call the
    keeping of them the giving of five great gifts: whoever refrains gives countless beings the gift
    of freedom from fear, enmity, and ill will, and enjoys that same unlimited freedom themselves.

    <em>Source: [Sāmaññaphala Sutta (DN 2)](https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato) - SuttaCentral; [Abhisanda Sutta (AN 8.39)](https://suttacentral.net/an8.39/en/sujato) - SuttaCentral</em>
  </summary>
