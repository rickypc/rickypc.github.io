### Daśa Cūla Dhāraṇī

<Phrase {...phrase} {...props} />

<details>
  <summary>
    Daśa Cūla Dhāraṇī, the Ten Short Prayers
    ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 十小咒), is a collection
    of short [esoteric Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajrayana)
    [dhāraṇīs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharani) for daily recitation. The ten are:

    > 1. **[Cintāmaṇi Cakravarti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cint%C4%81ma%E1%B9%87icakra)**
         Dhāraṇī ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [如意寶輪王陀羅尼](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%A6%82%E6%84%8F%E8%BC%AA%E8%A7%80%E9%9F%B3%E9%99%80%E7%BE%85%E5%B0%BC)):
         the wish-fulfilling wheel of the Cintāmaṇicakra form of Avalokiteśvara, who holds the
         wish-granting jewel and the wheel. It clears obstacles and fulfils wishes, and comes from
         the 如意寶輪王陀羅尼經 (T1081), translated by Yijing in 710.
    > 2. **Jvāla Mahāugra Dhāranī**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [消災吉祥神咒](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B6%88%E7%81%BD%E5%90%89%E7%A5%A5%E7%B6%93)):
         the blazing-radiance formula of the Buddha 熾盛光如來, recited to avert calamity and
         inauspicious conditions. Its scripture (T0963) was translated by Amoghavajra, who worked
         between 746 and 774.
    > 3. **Guṇaratna Śīla Dhāranī**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [功德寶山神咒](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%8A%9F%E5%BE%B7%E5%AF%B6%E5%B1%B1%E7%A5%9E%E5%92%92)):
         the jewel-mountain of merit formula. It comes down through the daily-recitation anthologies,
         the earliest combined witness being the Ming collection 諸經日誦集要 (J19nB044).
    > 4. **Mahā [Cundī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cundi_(Buddhism)) Durgā Dhāranī**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [準提神咒](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%87%86%E6%8F%90%E9%99%80%E7%BE%85%E5%B0%BC)):
         the mantra of Cundī, the pure goddess titled mother of the seven koṭis of buddhas. It
         purifies karma and removes obstructions, and its scripture (T1076) was translated by
         Amoghavajra, with an earlier version (T1075) by Vajrabodhi in 723.
    > 5. **Aparimitāyurjñāna Suviniścita Tejorājāya Dhāraṇī**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 聖無量壽決定光明王陀羅尼):
         the long-life formula of the Tathāgata 聖無量壽決定光明王, Holy Unmeasured Life of Decided
         Radiance. It is recited for longevity and the clearing of obstructive karma, and its
         scripture (T0937) was translated by 法天 in 973.
    > 6. **[Bhaiṣajyaguru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaisajyaguru) Vaiḍūrya Prabhāsa Tathāgata
         Abhiṣecanī Dhāraṇī**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [藥師灌頂真言](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%97%A5%E5%B8%AB%E7%81%8C%E9%A0%82%E7%9C%9F%E8%A8%80)):
         the Medicine Buddha's consecration mantra, recited for healing and for the removal of
         illness and karmic obstruction. It stands in the Medicine Buddha scriptures: the
         seven-buddha version (T0451), translated by Yijing in 707, and the single-buddha version
         (T0450), translated by Xuanzang in 650.
    > 7. **[Āryavalokiteśvarā Bodhisattva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalokite%C5%9Bvara)
         Vikurvāṇā Dhāraṇī**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [觀音靈感真言](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%A7%80%E9%9F%B3%E9%9D%88%E6%84%9F%E7%9C%9F%E8%A8%80)):
         the responsive-manifestation formula of Avalokiteśvara, which incorporates the six-syllable
         六字大明咒, <code>{avalokitesvaraPhrase.transliteration.children}</code>. It comes down
         through the daily-recitation anthologies, the earliest witness being the Ming collection
         諸經日誦集要 (J19nB044).
    > 8. **[Sapta Atītabuddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhahood#The_Seven_Buddhas_of_Antiquity)
         Karasaniya Dhāraṇī**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [七佛滅罪真言](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%B8%83%E4%BD%9B%E6%BB%85%E7%BD%AA%E7%9C%9F%E8%A8%80)):
         the absolution of the seven past buddhas. It is recited in the 方等三昧 repentance practice
         and stands in its manual, the 方等三昧行法 (T1940), which derives from the 大方等陀羅尼經
         (T1339), translated by 法眾 between 402 and 413. The name 七佛滅罪真言 is the one carried by
         the Ming daily-recitation anthologies.
    > 9. **[Sukhāvatīvyūha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longer_Sukh%C4%81vat%C4%ABvy%C5%ABha_S%C5%ABtra)
         Dhāraṇī**
         ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
         [拔一切業障根本得生淨土陀羅尼](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%BE%80%E7%94%9F%E5%92%92)):
         Amitābha's rebirth mantra, better known as 往生咒. It uproots obstructive karma and secures
         rebirth in Sukhāvatī; its scripture (T0368) was retranslated by Guṇabhadra between 435
         and 443.
    > 10. **Śrīdēvī Dhāraṇī**
          ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
          [大吉祥天女咒](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%90%89%E7%A5%A5%E5%A4%A9%E5%A5%B3#%E5%90%89%E7%A5%A5%E5%A4%A9%E5%A5%B3%E5%92%92)):
          the formula of 大吉祥天女, the goddess of fortune, invoked for wealth, the relief of
          poverty, and auspicious conditions. It comes from the eighth fascicle of the
          金光明最勝王經 (T0665), the chapter on her increase of wealth, translated by Yijing in 703.

    The set is preserved in the traditional daily-recitation collection 諸經日誦集要.

    <em>Source: [諸經日誦集要](https://cbetaonline.dila.edu.tw/JB044) - CBETA, J19nB044</em>
