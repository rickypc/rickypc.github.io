### Mañjuśrī Hṛdaya

<Phrase {...phrase} {...props} />

<details>
  <summary>
    Mañjuśrī is a bodhisattva associated with
    [wisdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praj%C3%B1%C4%81_(Buddhism)) and insight in Mahāyāna and
    Vajrayāna Buddhism, and his name is often translated as Gentle Glory. His heart mantra is
    `oṃ arapacana dhīḥ`, and this prayer opens the five
    [Dharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharma#Buddha's_teachings) doors, the syllables of the
    mantra each marking one such door. The syllable ā in ādya-anutpannatvād marks the insight that
    all dharmas are unproduced from the very beginning; ra in rajas, the insight that all dharmas
    are without stain; pa in paramārtha, the insight that all dharmas have been expounded in the
    ultimate sense; cya in cyavana, the insight that the decrease or rebirth of any dharma cannot be
    apprehended, since all dharmas neither decrease nor are reborn; and nā in nāma, the insight that
    the names of all dharmas have vanished, so that the essential nature behind names can be neither
    gained nor lost.

    This prayer is believed to enhance
    [wisdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praj%C3%B1%C4%81_(Buddhism)) and to improve skill in
    debating, memory, writing, and other
    [literary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_Buddhist_literature) abilities.

    <em>Source: [One Hundred and Eight Names of Mañjuśrī](https://84000.co/translation/toh642) - 84000: Translating the Words of the Buddha</em>
  </summary>
