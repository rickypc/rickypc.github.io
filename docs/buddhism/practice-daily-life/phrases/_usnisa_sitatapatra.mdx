### Uṣṇīṣa Sitātapatrā Hṛdaya

<Phrase {...phrase} {...props} />

<details>
  <summary>
    [Sitātapatrā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitatapatra), the 'White Parasol', is a powerful
    protector against supernatural danger in [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism),
    revered in both the [Mahāyāna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana) and
    [Vajrayāna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajrayana) traditions. Her name, sitātapatrā, means
    white parasol or white umbrella. She is believed to emanate from the
    [Uṣṇīṣa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ushnisha) of [Gautama
    Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha) while he was abiding in deep samādhi,
    manifesting as an independent deity whose mantrapada offers protection from calamities,
    malevolent forces, and harmful influences. Brilliant white and radiating compassion, she is
    often depicted with a white parasol and, in her most elaborate form, with a thousand heads,
    arms, and eyes that watch over and safeguard beings.

    Reciting her dhāraṇī is traditionally said to avert obstacles, purify defilements, and ensure a
    favorable rebirth, including the aspiration to be reborn in
    [Amitābha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amit%C4%81bha)'s [pure
    land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_land) of
    [Sukhāvatī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhavati). Sitātapatrā's practice is widely used for
    protection, healing, and establishing protective boundaries, making her one of the most
    important guardians in Vajrayāna Buddhism.

    <em>Source: [Sitātapatrā Born from the Uṣṇīṣa of All Tathāgatas](https://84000.co/translation/toh590) - 84000 Translating the Words of the Buddha</em>
  </summary>
