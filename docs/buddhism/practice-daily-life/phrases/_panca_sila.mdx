### Pañcaśīla

<Phrase {...phrase} {...props} />

<details>
  <summary>
    Pañcaśīla, the [five
    precepts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_precepts#In_P%C4%81li_tradition), are the basic
    training rules undertaken by lay Buddhists, consisting of abstaining from killing, stealing,
    sexual misconduct, false speech, and intoxication. They are the simplest way to avoid creating
    new karmic debts and serve as gentle reminders to stay kind and careful, and they are recited as
    a commitment to ethical conduct and non-harm.

    Keeping these precepts keeps the karmic balance sheet clean - fewer new debts, fewer
    complications, and a quieter heart, and it is traditionally held to protect the mind, reduce
    remorse, and support meditation.

    > 1. **Don't hurt living beings**: every creature wants to live. Be gentle
         with people, animals, even tiny bugs.
    > 2. **Don't take what isn't yours**: if it doesn't belong to you, leave
         it. Sharing is good, stealing is not.
    > 3. **Don't break trust in love**: be honest and kind in friendships and
         relationships. Don't cause harm with your choices.
    > 4. **Don't lie**: words can heal or hurt. Speak the truth, and use words
         to help, not harm.
    > 5. **Don't cloud your mind**: stay clear and mindful. Avoid things that
         make you lose control or forget kindness.

    <em>Source: [Sāmaññaphala Sutta (DN 2)](https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato) - SuttaCentral</em>
