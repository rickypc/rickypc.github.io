### Mahāpratisarā Vidyā Dhāraṇī

<Phrase {...phrase} {...props} />

<details>
  <summary>
    The **Mahāpratisarā Vidyā Dhāraṇī** is one of the most revered protective formulas in the
    Buddhist world, centered on the goddess
    [Mahāpratisarā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mah%C4%81pratisar%C4%81), a radiant embodiment of
    safeguard, healing, and karmic purification. Regarded as the "[Great
    Amulet](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=The_Great_Amulet)", this dhāraṇī occupies a
    central place within the [Pañcarakṣā](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Pancha_Raksha)
    (Five Great Protectors), a collection of scriptures believed to shield practitioners from
    illness, misfortune, and untimely death.

    The five protectors, each a scripture and the goddess it personifies, are:

    > 1. **Mahāpratisarā** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
          大隨求): the Great Amulet herself, invoked to grant wishes, purify karma, and shield whoever
          recites, writes, or carries the dhāraṇī. Her scriptures include the 大隨求陀羅尼經 (T1153),
          translated by Amoghavajra, and the 佛說隨求即得大自在陀羅尼神呪經 (T1154), translated by
          寶思惟 in 693.
    > 2. **Mahāsahasrapramardinī** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
          大千摧碎): the Destroyer of the Great Trichiliocosm, invoked against calamity, nāgas, and
          epidemic. Its scripture is the 佛說守護大千國土經 (T0999), translated by 施護 in 980.
    > 3. **Mahāmāyūrī** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
          大孔雀明王): the Great Peacock Queen of Mantras, invoked against snakebite, poison, and
          disease. Its scripture is the 佛母大孔雀明王經 (T0982), translated by Amoghavajra.
    > 4. **Mahāśītavatī** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
          大寒林): the goddess of the Great Cool Grove, invoked against harmful spirits and disease.
          Its scripture is the 大寒林聖難拏陀羅尼經 (T1392), translated by 法天 in 973.
    > 5. **Mahāmantrānusāriṇī** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters):
          大秘咒隨持): the Great Upholder of the Secret Mantra, invoked for protection and the growth
          of wholesome roots. It is identified with the 佛說大護明大陀羅尼經 (T1048), translated by
          法天 in 973.

    Each of the five comes down in its own scripture.

    Often depicted as a **Vidyārājñī** ("Queen of Mantras"), Mahāpratisarā is invoked for her power
    to dispel obstacles, neutralize malevolent forces, and grant well-being to those who recite,
    write, or carry her dhāraṇī. The mantra itself contains potent syllables - such as
    {<Words {...trikayaPhrase} />} - associated with illumination, protection, and the cutting of
    negativity. Across South Asia, Tibet, and East Asia, this dhāraṇī has been inscribed on amulets,
    printed on talismans, and recited in rituals for centuries, earning a reputation as one of
    Buddhism's most effective and compassionate protective formulas.
